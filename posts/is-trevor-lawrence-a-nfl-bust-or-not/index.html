Trevor Lawrence: Bust, or Victim of Expectations?

When Trevor Lawrence was selected first overall in the 2021 NFL Draft, he was widely regarded as one of the safest quarterback prospects in recent memory. After winning a national championship at Clemson and establishing himself as one of the most efficient quarterbacks in college football, expectations surrounding his professional career were substantial.

Five seasons into his NFL career, however, the conversation has evolved. While Lawrence has displayed many of the traits associated with a franchise quarterback, he has yet to establish himself among the NFL’s elite. As a result, questions have emerged about whether he has fallen short of expectations enough to be considered a draft bust.

Evaluating those questions requires examining the expectations Lawrence carried into the NFL, comparing his professional production to quarterbacks commonly viewed as draft busts, and assessing where he fits among current NFL starters.

The Expectations Were Built at Clemson Before determining whether Trevor Lawrence should be considered a bust, it is important to understand why expectations were so high. Lawrence entered the NFL as one of the most accomplished quarterbacks in college football, leading Clemson to a national championship while producing elite passing numbers throughout his collegiate career.

The chart below compares Lawrence’s career passer rating to several of Clemson’s most accomplished quarterbacks. If Lawrence were viewed as a generational prospect entering the NFL, his performance should stand out even among some of the most successful quarterbacks in program history.

library(tidyverse)
library(nflreadr)
library(gt)

passingleaders <- read_csv ("clemson_passing_leaders.csv")

bustqbs <- read_csv("bust_qbs.csv")

season_stats <- load_player_stats(2021: 2026)

passingleaders <- passingleaders |> 
  mutate(Player = gsub("\\*", "", Player)
  )



topqbs <- passingleaders |> 
  filter(
    Player %in% c(
      "Trevor Lawrence", 
      "Deshaun Watson", 
      "Tajh Boyd", 
      "Cade Klubnik", 
      "Charlie Whitehurst"
      )
    )




trevor <- topqbs |> 
  filter(Player == "Trevor Lawrence")

clemsonlegends <- topqbs |> 
  filter(Player != "Trevor Lawrence")




ggplot() +

Lawrence finished with the highest passer rating among this group of Clemson quarterbacks. While players such as Deshaun Watson and Tajh Boyd also enjoyed highly successful collegiate careers, Lawrence’s efficiency helped establish him as one of the most highly regarded quarterback prospects of the modern era.

His accomplishments at Clemson created expectations that very few NFL prospects have faced. Those expectations continue to shape how his professional career is evaluated.

Does Lawrence Belong in the Bust Conversation? Despite entering the NFL with enormous expectations, Lawrence has yet to reach the level many anticipated. As a result, some analysts and fans have labeled his career a “draft bust.”

To evaluate whether the data supports that criticism, Lawrence’s passing touchdown production was compared with that of several quarterbacks commonly recognized as notable NFL draft busts.

At first glance, Lawrence’s professional career may seem disappointing compared with the expectations that accompanied him into the NFL. However, comparing his production to several notable draft busts presents a different picture.

Lawrence has already thrown more touchdown passes than quarterbacks such as Johnny Manziel, Josh Rosen, Trey Lance, and Paxton Lynch. Unlike many of those players, he has remained a consistent NFL starter and continued to produce at a meaningful level. Although the data does not suggest Lawrence has developed into a superstar, it does demonstrate that his production significantly exceeds that of many quarterbacks commonly considered draft failures.

How Does Lawrence Compare to Current NFL Starters?

While Lawrence has outperformed many quarterbacks labeled draft busts, that comparison alone does not determine whether he has been successful. A more meaningful evaluation compares his performance to quarterbacks currently starting across the NFL.

The chart below compares Lawrence’s passing touchdown production to a group of established veterans and some of the league’s most successful quarterbacks. If Lawrence truly belongs in the bust category, his production would be expected to fall well below that of his peers.

The data indicates that Lawrence has not reached the level of the NFL’s elite quarterbacks. Players such as Patrick Mahomes, Josh Allen, and Lamar Jackson have produced at a significantly higher level and have established themselves among the league’s premier players.

However, Lawrence’s production remains comparable to a number of successful NFL starters. His touchdown totals place him much closer to productive starting quarterbacks than to players commonly viewed as draft busts.

While Lawrence has not developed into the transcendent player many projected when he entered the league, the data suggests he remains a capable franchise quarterback and a productive NFL starter.

Conclusion Based on the available data, Trevor Lawrence should not be considered a draft bust.

Lawrence entered the NFL carrying expectations that few prospects in league history have faced. His success at Clemson established him as one of the most accomplished quarterbacks in college football and created the expectations that he would quickly become one of the NFL’s starters.

Ultimately, the evidence suggests that Lawrence’s career has been shaped more by extraordinary expectations than poor performance. Although he has not reached the level of quarterbacks such as Patrick Mahomes or Lamar Jackson, the data demonstrates that he is far closer to a productive franchise quarterback than to a draft bust.
